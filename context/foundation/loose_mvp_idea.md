WorldViewMap (WVMap) is a specialized web platform designed for mapping individual belief systems, structuring complex reasoning, and identifying the exact roots of intellectual disagreements.

The fundamental philosophy of the platform is to move public discourse away from emotional, chaotic shouting matches toward collaborative truth-seeking and epistemological accountability.

The system maps reasoning using a hierarchical structure of interconnected, atomic propositions called Claims and the Logical Relations that bind them together.

It adapts classic argumentation theory (Toulmin model) into an intuitive user interface, allowing anyone to clearly visualize how their conclusions depend on specific premises.

In the primary use case, a user utilizes the platform in isolation to systematically input their own worldviews, stating their claims and establishing the specific dependencies between them.

A user declares their Claims (theses they hold to be true) and anchors them to foundational Data (verifiable facts or baseline assumptions).

They then explicitly define the Warrants (the logical bridges), answering the vital question: "Why exactly does this specific foundation prove this conclusion?"

Once the map is drawn, the author must execute a "Personal Commitment" by explicitly signing off on every atomic statement and logical link in their tree.

This act of commitment allows the system to analyze the author's own internal consistency, ensuring their worldview does not contain unacknowledged contradictions or circular reasoning.

Once finalized, the user can Publish their worldview map to the web, initially as a read-only artifact.

Public figures, scientists, and educators can use these published maps to transparently show their entire line of reasoning on a specific topic, such as climate change, ethics, or economics.

The second phase of the platform introduces the interactive Dispute and Challenge mechanics.

A published map owner can choose to open their tree to a specific individual for a private 1-to-1 clarification, or open it publicly to any challenger on the internet.

When a challenger enters the map, they cannot modify the original author’s text; instead, they must audit the tree element by element.

For every single Claim, Data node, and Warrant bridge, the challenger must explicitly choose either [Accept] or [Challenge].

This interaction forces the challenger to acknowledge verified facts, preventing them from attacking strawman arguments or shifting the goalposts.

As the challenger progresses, the platform’s core domain logic performs a digital subtraction of all mutually agreed-upon nodes.

The system automatically filters out and hides the "common ground" where both participants selected [Accept].

It then dynamically isolates and highlights the exact node or logical bridge where the first [Challenge] occurred, exposing the precise Crux of their divergence.

By separating agreed-upon facts from fundamental value differences, WorldViewMap clears intellectual confusion for the individual and reveals the true pivot point of any public debate.
